1. WHAT THIS SYSTEM IS ABOUT (LANDSCAPE)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Aquilala is an IRRIGATORS ASSOCIATION: a group of farmers sharing an
  irrigation facility (canal, pump, small reservoir). Farmer-members grow
  seasonal crops, mostly PALAY (rice) with corn and vegetables where water
  allows.
- The system digitizes three core loops:
  a) LEARNING: farmers get AI-driven recommendations and input formulas
     (product x rate per hectare per season) from store owners.
  b) BUYING: farmers request quotations / cost estimates from stores; stores
     manage inventory.
  c) REPORTING: farmers track crops through growth stages and submit HARVEST
     REPORTS (actual yield + actual expenses) afterward.
- Analytics pages summarize these loops for the admin.

2. UNITS & MEASUREMENTS (WHY "METRIC TONS")
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- HECTARE (ha): standard area unit. 1 ha = 10,000 m² = about 2.5 acres
  (~1 football field is roughly 0.7 ha). Typical smallholder rice farms are
  0.5–3 ha.
- METRIC TON (MT): 1 MT = 1,000 kilograms = ~2,204 lbs.
- YIELD is conventionally reported as metric tons PER HECTARE (t/ha or the
  Philippine-derived /ha). Examples of national averages/philosophy:
    - Rice (palay): ~3.5–4.5 t/ha for irrigated lowland.
      Our "Total Yield" card = SUM of actual_yield_metric_tons (total weight
      across all reports, NOT divided by ha).
    - Corn: ~3–5 t/ha (hybrid yellow, irrigated/fertilized).
- Why MT? It's the international + DA standard for bulk crops. For vegetables
  or small plots, kg is more natural. A 0.0 MT reading simply means a report
  recorded less than ~50 kg, or none was submitted yet.
- Other units common in the field:
    - CAVAN (rice): ~50 kg of palay (unhusked paddy).
    - SACK/BAG: ~50 kg (fertilizer commonly in 50-kg bags).
    - LITERS PER HECTARE (L/ha) or MILLILITERS per 16-L knapsack sprayer for
      liquid pesticides/foliar.
    - KILOGRAMS PER HECTARE (kg/ha) for granule fertilizers and seed rates.
- SEED RATE (how much seed to plant per ha):
    - Rice: ~40–60 kg/ha for transplanted, 80–120 kg/ha for direct seeding.
    - Corn: ~15–20 kg/ha.  Vegetables: varies widely (pechay ~1–2 kg/ha,
      eggplant ~200–400 g/ha of hybrid seed).

3. WET & DRY SEASON — WHY THE SYSTEM TAGS EVERYTHING BY SEASON
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Philippines has two main monsoons that define the cropping calendar:
- WET SEASON ("tagulan") — roughly JUNE to OCTOBER.
  Brought by the southwest monsoon (HABAGAT). Heavy rains, high humidity.
  Favors rice; but typhoons/flooding and more pest pressure (bacterial leaf
  blight, blast) are risks.
- DRY SEASON ("tag-araw") — roughly NOVEMBER to MAY.
  Brought by the northeast monsoon (AMIHAN) - cooler mornings, then hot
  drought stretch March–May. Higher solar radiation. Generally HIGHER rice
  yields and cleaner harvests; but irrigation water becomes critical.

Why it matters to your data model:
- Aquilala is IRRIGATED, so farmers can plant in BOTH seasons
  (irrigated): dry-season rice then wet-season rice, or rice-corn/vegetable
  rotation. Rainfed farms can typically only manage a wet-season crop.
- Some crops are season-gated:
    - Wet season: rice (especially rainfed/transplanted), sweet potato,
      ginger, eggplant (starts wet), corn for silage.
    - Dry season: corn (yellow/white), munggo, watermelon/melon, garlic,
      onion, tomato, red pepper, peanut, and rice (irrigated).
- The "Crops by Season" donut and the season tag on quotations/formulas exist
  because INPUT RATES legitimately differ between seasons: e.g., wet-season
  rice needs more fertilizer (rain leaches nutrients) and more fungicide;
  dry-season often uses less insecticide but more water management.
- PHILIPPINE CLIMATE HAZARDS to keep in mind for explanations:
  EL NIÑO = drought / delayed rains -> dry-season water stress, crop failure.
  LA NIÑA = above-normal rain -> flooding, seedling damage, higher disease.

4. RICE (PALAY) — THE CORE CROP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Growth stages used in this system (aligns with what farmers know):
    LAND PREPARATION -> SEEDING/SOWING -> VEGETATIVE GROWTH ->
    FLOWERING/FRUITING (panicle initiation -> heading) -> RIPENING ->
    HARVESTED  (FAILED = crop lost, e.g., drought/flood/pest).
- Duration: lowland palay ~ 100–120 days (short varieties ~105 days).
  Mapping: land prep ~2 weeks; vegetative ~40–50 days; reproductive
  (flowering) ~30–40 days; ripening ~25–35 days.
- Watering needs: irrigated lowland keeps 2–5 cm standing water during
  vegetative growth, drains before harvest.
- Fertilizer typical for 1 ha irrigated dry-season rice:
    Basal (transplant): Complete 14-14-14  ~100–150 kg/ha
    Urea (46-0-0) splits: ~2–3 bags around tillering + panicle initiation
    TOTAL N roughly 90–130 kg/ha.
  Advisories change by soil test / leaf color chart (LCC).
- Pests/diseases to reference: stem borer, leaf folder, rice blast
  (Pyricularia), bacterial leaf blight, tungro virus.
- Harvest: palay at ~20–24% moisture, dried to ~14% for storage.
  Rough rule: 1 ha of good palay ≈ 80–90 cavans * 50 kg ≈ 4–4.5 MT.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. CORN
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Types:
    - YELLOW CORN (feed/industrial) — almost always HYBRID.
    - WHITE CORN (as food "mais") — hybrid or open-pollinated (e.g., IPB Var 6).
    - SWEET CORN (table/whole) — short-season, harvested soft-cob.
    - GLUTINOUS/CORNFLIK — native types for "binatog"/"tamales".
- Days to harvest: ~90–110 days (grain), ~75–90 days (sweet, fresh).
- Season: dry season preferred (needs less water; warm sunny weather).
- Seeding: 1 seed per hill, 75 cm between rows, 20–25 cm between hills.
- Fertilizer (hybrid yellow): 14-14-14 basal ~100–150 kg/ha + urea splits.
  Careful: N beyond optimum causes lodging and poorer grain fill.

6. VEGETABLES & OTHER CROPS (SHORT EXTRACTS FOR REFERENCE)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- EGGPLANT ("talong") : dry-season crop; ~4–5 months; trellised.
- TOMATO: dry season; temperature ~20–28°C nights cool. Highly disease-prone
  (wilts) — rotation advised.
- AMPALAYA (bitter gourd): trellised; wet to dry transition; long harvest window.
- PECHAY / BOK CHOY: cool~warm, fast (25–35 days); heavy N feeder, leafy.
- GARLIC & ONION: strictly dry-season (Ilocos); daylength + cool dry conditions.
- WATERMELON / MELON: dry season, deep sandy-loam, irrigation critical at
  fruit set.
- PEANUT / MUNGGO (mungbean): dry season, low inputs, soil-improving (fix N).
- GINGER ("luya"): 8–10 months, moist fertile soil, prefers wet-season start.
- SWEET POTATO ("kamote"): drought-tolerant, low input, ~4 months.

7. HYBRID vs INBRED vs OPEN-POLLINATED vs GMO — "SEED CLASSES"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SEED TYPE                | WHAT IT IS                          | KEY TRADE-OFF
-------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------
INBRED (certified,       | First filial of naturally           | Cheaper; farmer can
 pure-line)              | self-pollinated selection           | save seed; lower yield
-------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------
HYBRID (F1)              | Cross of two inbred parents         | Higher yield & uniform;
                         |                                    | seed must be RE-BOUGHT
                         |                                    | every season (saving
                         |                                    | seed = quality loss)
-------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------
OPEN-POLLINATED (OPV)    | Varieties that pollinate freely     | Adaptable, saveable,
                         |                                    | moderate yield
-------------------------|-------------------------------------|-------------
GMO (GM/Bt)              | Gene-modified (e.g., Bt corn)       | Pest-resistant trait;
                         |                                    | regulatory + market
                         |                                    | considerations

COMMON nomenclatures to encounter in stores:
- "Hybrid corn seed" vs "OPV corn seed" — price difference is large.
- Rice variety names like NSIC Rc222/Rc160/Rc216 (inbred) vs commercial
  hybrids (e.g., SL-8H, US-2H, Mestiso series) — hybrid rice yields ~15–25%
  higher but needs more inputs + careful water management.
- Bt CORN: carries a gene against corn borer larvae; marketed for yellow feed
  corn.
- FOUNDATION vs CERTIFIED seed: seed classes in certified seed production —
  breeders' seed -> foundation -> registered -> certified. A label matters
  for germination % and purity.

IMPACT ON YOUR SYSTEM: "crop variety / variety name" on the crop card and
seed-type awareness helps explain price differences in quotations. Hybrids
are bought every season -> that is recurring store revenue and recurring
farmer input expense.

8. PLANTING CONDITIONS — WHAT VARIES AND WHY IT MATTERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four big levers: WATER, TEMPERATURE, SUNLIGHT, SOIL.

- WATER:
    - Irrigated vs rainfed is THE biggest yield divider.
    - Rice = water-loving during growth; corn/vegetables = sensitive to
      flooding.
    - Timing of water stress matters: flowering stage is the most
      drought-sensitive in rice and corn.
- TEMPERATURE:
    - Rice: 20–35°C; stress spikes reduce fertility (spikelet sterility).
    - Corn: warm 20–30°C; needs warm nights for grain fill.
    - High night temps during flowering reduce yield in both.
- SUNLIGHT/DAYLENGTH:
    - Rice is mostly day-neutral; crops like onion/garlic need specific
      photoperiods -> "short-day" crops grown in dry season.
- SOIL:
    - pH 5.5–7.0 ideal for most crops; rice tolerates slightly acidic wet
      soils (4.5–6.5).
    - Texture: clay-loam for rice; sandy-loam for root crops/watermelon.
    - Organic matter improves water holding & nutrient release.

PLANTING SEASONALITY (rule of thumb table for the panel):
    Crop        | Best window (PH)         | Notes
    ------------|--------------------------|---------------------------------
    Rice (palay)| Wet & Dry (irrigated)    | Dry season = higher yield
    Corn        | Dry                     | Hybrid yellow for feed
    Eggplant    | Wet start -> Dry       | Long season, trellised
    Tomato      | Dry (cool nights)       | Wilt-sensitive, rotate
    Garlic/Onion| Dry (strictly)          | Daylength-gated
    Watermelon  | Dry                     | Needs irrigation at fruit set
    Pechay      | Dry                                           | Fast, leafy
    Peanut/Mung | Dry                     | Low input, fixes N

9. INPUTS (STORE CATALOG) — WHAT FARMERS BUY
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- FERTILIZERS: complete (14-14-14, 17-17-17), UREA (46-0-0), MOP/Amonia
  (0-0-60 / 20-0-0-24S ammonium sulfate), ammonium phosphate (16-20-0),
  foliar/liquid (macro + micro), organic fertilizer (chicken manure, compost).
  NPK = the three numbers: Nitrogen-Phosphorus-Potassium.
- PESTICIDES (categorized in your system): INSECTICIDES (for stem borer,
  leaf folder), FUNGICIDES (blast, blight), HERBICIDES (weed control: pre- and
  post-emergence), MOLLUSCICIDES (golden apple snail).
- GROWTH REGULATORS & FOLIARS: micronutrient mixes, biostimulants.
- TOOLS/OTHER: knapsack sprayer, trellis materials, irrigation pipes,
  protective gear.
- Categories in your products table map to: Fertilizer, Seeds, Pesticide,
  Herbicide, Tools, Other — pesticides vs herbicides are separate because
  they act on insects/fungi vs plants.

10. QUOTATIONS = COST ESTIMATES (NOT REVENUE)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- A QUOTATION in this system is the STORE OWNER's cost estimate for a
  farmer's input list: product x quantity x price summarized, optionally
  split by season (wet/dry) because rates differ per season.
- It is NOT a sale/invoice and creates NO revenue tie-in. Keep this framing
  in every dashboard/analytics explanation.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. HARVEST REPORTS & "ACTUAL" vs "ESTIMATED"
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The LEARNING/FORMULA side is ESTIMATED (planned inputs and rates).
- The HARVEST REPORT captures ACTUALS after the season:
    crop_type, actual_yield_metric_tons, actual_total_expenses.
- Comparing actual vs estimated is how a farmer learns (and how the system
  refines recommendations). Actual expenses rarely equal the quotation:
  prices move, sprays are skipped, damage forces re-entry, etc.

12. GLOSSARY (USE CONSISTENTLY IN UI/EXPLAINERS)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Palay       - unhusked rice (paddy)
Bigas       - milled/white rice
Bayanihan   - not agronomic; just note cooperative spirit of associations
Amihan      - NE monsoon (dry-season cooler winds)
Habagat     - SW monsoon (wet-season rains)
Tag-araw    - dry season;  Tagulan - wet season
Irrigated   - water supplied by canal/pump (Aquilala's case)
Rainfed     - depending on rainfall alone
T/ha, MT/ha - metric tons per hectare (yield standard)
Basal       - fertilizer applied at planting/transplanting
Topdress    - fertilizer applied later in the season (split dosing)
Cavans/ha   - rice yield in 50-kg units
Transplant  - raising seedlings then planting out (vs direct seeding)
Lodging     - crops falling over (weak straw / excess N / wind)
Tiller       - productive shoot of rice (more tillers = more panicles)
Panicle     - rice "head" bearing the grains
GDD/PH      - growing degree days / photoperiod (advanced concepts)
NSIC        - National Seed Industry Council (variety registry)
LCC         - Leaf Color Chart (N management tool)

13. QUICK "HELP ME EXPLAIN THIS CARD" NOTES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- "Total Yield 0.0 MT": no harvest reports recorded yet; small yields show
  as 0.0 because display is in whole MT. Consider auto-switching to kg when
  actual < 1 MT (suggested improvement).
- "0.0 MT" vs "—": you could show "—" instead of 0.0 when there are zero
  reports at all, to avoid implying a real reading of zero.
